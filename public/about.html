About Tallyhouse

Tallyhouse is a personal spending dashboard. It reads your bank and brokerage accounts through Lunchflow, sorts each transaction with keyword rules you control, and shows where your money went, month by month.

Set up whatever categories suit you, and mark any of them to be tracked on their own, apart from everyday spending. Transfers between your own accounts are ignored, refunds are matched against the purchase they reverse, and foreign-currency payments are converted at the rate from the day they happened.

An AI review reads each transaction to suggest a category, flag amounts that look unusual for a payee, and point out merchants that are widely reported as scams. It runs on your own OpenAI key, so what it costs is visible to you and billed to you — and you decide how much of it to buy: setup asks how far back to review and whether the reviewer may look payees up on the web, with an estimate of what your answers come to, and both stay editable in Settings.

Or you decide not to buy any of it. The OpenAI key is optional: leave it out and there is no review, nothing about your transactions ever reaches a model, and everything else works the same. Setup will offer, once, to propose your starting categories from your payee names on Tallyhouse's own key — say yes or no, and add a key in Settings later if you change your mind.

No tracking, no advertising, no analytics — and therefore no cookie banner to dismiss, because the only cookie is the one that keeps you signed in. Your API keys are encrypted at rest, and Tallyhouse only ever reads your accounts: it cannot move money. The privacy page spells out exactly what is stored and where it goes.

Tallyhouse is built and reviewed with Claude Opus 5, including passes aimed squarely at security: rate limits on everything that reaches a third party, a strict content-security policy, and a hard ceiling on what any single request can make the server do. Those reviews keep finding and fixing real things, which is rather the point — it is an AI code review rather than a professional audit, so take it as evidence of care rather than a promise that nothing is wrong.

You can export everything you have here as a single file, or delete your account and all of its data, at any time from Settings.

Setting up takes a Lunchflow API key, and an OpenAI API key if you want the review — the setup page explains where to get each one.
