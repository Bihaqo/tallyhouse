A typical account

Counted over accounts that finished setup — an account that stopped at the key form is a zero in every column below, and including it would just be the funnel again. Median and average are both here because one heavy user drags a mean upward on an instance this size; where they disagree, the median is the typical account and the average is somebody's outlier. Neither is a person: this panel holds no per-account rows and no email addresses, by choice rather than by absence.

Feedback

What this panel cannot tell you

Every number above is read from data the app already had to keep. There is no event log and no page-view record, because the privacy policy says "no analytics, no tracking" and a first-party cookieless one would still be analytics. So:

  • Who anybody is, in any of the numbers. That is a choice, not a gap: the database knows every address, and the queries behind them decline to select it. The feedback above is the deliberate exception, and the distinction is the whole point — somebody wrote that message to you, rather than being counted while they used the app. Anything else that genuinely needs one account — a support request, a deletion — goes through psql, where it costs a deliberate act instead of a glance. With only a handful of accounts, note that an average is never far from an individual either.
  • Which page someone abandoned is not recorded anywhere, and no reconstruction of it exists.
  • Last activity is not available. It looks like it should be — transactions_cache.cached_at updates when the dashboard loads — but the background sweep refreshes it hourly for every onboarded account, so it measures the scheduler. Last sign-in is real, and that is what is shown.
  • Time on site, clicks and navigation order were never collected.

Adding any of those means recording behaviour, which would need the privacy policy and the About page reworded first — the claim there is unqualified, and it is not made true by avoiding cookies.
